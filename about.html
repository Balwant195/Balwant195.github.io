About Me

My journey from Physics to Artificial Intelligence.

Why Physics?

My academic journey began with Physics because I was fascinated by the idea that a few mathematical principles could explain a vast range of natural phenomena—from the motion of planets to the behavior of atoms. During my undergraduate and postgraduate studies, I developed a strong foundation in mathematics, probability, statistics, optimization, and scientific reasoning.

Transition to Computer Science

While studying physics, I became increasingly interested in computation. I realized that modern science relies heavily on algorithms and data, which motivated me to pursue an M.Tech in Computer Science (Data Science) at the Indian Statistical Institute.

There I worked on multilingual natural language processing, machine learning, deep learning, and statistical learning methods, eventually specializing in transformer-based language models for sentiment analysis of code-mixed Indian languages.

Professional Experience

My industry experience at Zee Entertainment Enterprises allowed me to work on practical AI systems used by business teams. I helped build a conversational business intelligence platform using Azure AI Services, GPT-4, SQL generation, and Google BigQuery. I also developed ETL pipelines using Apache Airflow, REST APIs, and cloud data warehouses, gaining experience in data engineering alongside applied machine learning.

Research

As a Junior Research Fellow, I worked on privacy-preserving Federated Learning under a SERB-sponsored research project focused on secure AI for healthcare cyber-physical systems. My research explored how client- specific model pruning and differential privacy can improve both communication efficiency and privacy during collaborative model training.

Where I Want to Go

Although my current work focuses on machine learning and data engineering, my long-term research interests lie in Complex Systems, Collective Behaviour, Network Science, Swarm Intelligence, and the emergence of intelligence in natural and artificial systems.

I am particularly interested in understanding how simple local interactions give rise to complex global behaviour—from bird flocking and ant colonies to social networks and large language models. I hope to build computational models that help explain these phenomena while also contributing to practical AI systems.
