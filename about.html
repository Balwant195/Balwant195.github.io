About Me

My journey from Physics to Artificial Intelligence—and beyond.

Curiosity Came First

I have always been fascinated by understanding how the world works. My interest began with mathematics and physics, where elegant equations could explain everything from planetary motion to the behavior of atoms. Studying Physics taught me not only scientific concepts but also how to reason carefully, question assumptions, and build mathematical models of complex systems.

During this time I developed strong foundations in linear algebra, probability, statistics, optimization, and scientific programming—all of which later became essential tools in machine learning.

Transition to Computer Science

While pursuing Physics, I gradually became more interested in computation. I realized that many modern scientific discoveries were no longer driven solely by equations but by algorithms capable of learning from data.

This realization led me to pursue an M.Tech in Computer Science (Data Science) at the Indian Statistical Institute, Kolkata, where I studied machine learning, deep learning, natural language processing, computer vision, image processing, statistics, and data engineering.

Building AI Systems in Industry

At Zee Entertainment Enterprises Limited, I worked as an Associate Data Scientist where I contributed to two major areas: conversational AI and data engineering.

I helped build a conversational Business Intelligence platform that allowed users to query enterprise data using natural language. The system combined Azure Speech-to-Text, GPT-4, SQL generation, and Google BigQuery to make analytics accessible without requiring SQL knowledge.

Alongside this, I developed and maintained production ETL pipelines using Apache Airflow, Python, REST APIs, and Google BigQuery, and supported the migration of enterprise data workflows from AWS to Google Cloud Platform.

Research in Federated Learning

As a Junior Research Fellow at Netaji Subhash University of Technology, I worked on privacy-preserving Federated Learning for healthcare cyber-physical systems under a SERB-sponsored research project.

My research explored personalized federated learning using client- specific model pruning based on the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis together with Differential Privacy. I designed a secure communication mechanism that concealed pruning patterns using Gaussian noise while reducing communication overhead between clients and the central server.

This work resulted in a research manuscript currently submitted for publication.

Where I'm Going

Although my professional work today focuses on machine learning and data engineering, my long-term research interests extend much further.

I am fascinated by Complex Systems—how simple local interactions give rise to intelligent global behaviour. Examples include bird flocking, fish schooling, ant colonies, traffic flow, social networks, financial markets, biological systems, and even large language models.

My goal is to contribute to the scientific understanding of collective behaviour and emergent intelligence while continuing to build practical AI systems that solve real-world problems.
